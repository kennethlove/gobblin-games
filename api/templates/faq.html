Gameplay

the shape of a game

Q01What is Gobblin Games?

Goblins with deterministic brains battle it out until one team is left standing. You set up the game and draft the teams; the arena runs the days; everyone watches.

Q02What does "deterministic brains" mean?

A goblin reacts the same way to the same state. What changes is the state: almost every state change carries some randomness, so behaviour varies from occasion to occasion and no two games play out the same.

Q03How does a game end?

When only one team remains. Until then the arena keeps raising the sun and running days.

Q04How many days is a game?

As many as it takes. The total only becomes known once the game is over — a running game shows where you are, not where it ends.

Q05What do I do while a game runs?

Watch. The in-game page streams events as they happen, and every finished day stays open as a recap you can page back through.

Goblins

making them, keeping them

Q06What is a goblin?

A name, a clan, a portrait, character stats you set at creation, and a record the arena keeps up to date.

Q07Can a goblin play two games at once?

No. One game at a time. The goblin is yours again when that game ends.

Q08What happens when a goblin goes down?

Out for that game. Afterwards the goblin is revived, with a boon or a penalty attached to the revival — which one lands is part of the fun.

Q09Can I edit my goblin's record?

No. Wins, losses, eliminations and revivals come from the arena's database. Character stats are yours to set; the record is earned.

Games & players

setting one up

Q10How many teams and goblins can a game have?

Yours to set — team counts and team sizes both vary. The classic default is 8 teams of 3.

Q11How many players can join one game?

Up to 128, capped by the game's goblins — everyone controls at least one. If players don't fill every slot, the leftovers can be played by the CPU.

Q12What is the difference between private and public?

A private game runs on an invite link you share with friends — only people with the link can see it. A public game sits in the open list where anyone can find it.

Q13Who can delete a game?

The host who created it, and only after a confirmation step. Everyone else gets a view action and nothing more.

Running & watching

while the sun comes up

Q14Who decides when the game moves on?

The host. Advancing the game starts the next day, and that day's events stream in live for everyone watching — host and spectators alike.

Q15How do I follow the action?

Three ways at once. The day strip pages through the game one day at a time, the day's timeline runs top to bottom across twelve numbered periods, and the arena map shows where every goblin is standing.
